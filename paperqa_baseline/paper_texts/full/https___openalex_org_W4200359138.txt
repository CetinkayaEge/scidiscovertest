Title: Big Data for Better Tourism Policy, Management, and Sustainable Recovery from COVID-19
Authors: Natalia Bayona, Hernan Epstein, Dirk Glaesser, Aleli Rosario, Reza Vaez‐Zadeh, Eric Van Zant, B, A Babu, M Brezina, A Chuenniran, A Sattaburuth, P Chow, Y De Montjoye, L Radaelli, V Singh, A Pentland, M Epler Wood, M Milstein, K Ahamed-Broadhurst, B Faber, C Gaubert, A Garg, M Gauche, M Ghotkar, P Rokde, F Johns, C Compton, W Wobcke, L Kihara, J Lee, M Kang, P Lee, J Lim, K Matthews, A Min, D Muse, J Myers, K Whiting, Phuket The, News, E Russel, M Parsons, E Saluveer, A Sharon, R Shuman, F Paramita, K Stock, H Guesgen, D Svantesson, M Sweeney, J lvarez, K Tanaka, T Tay, C Taylor, S Thompson, C Warzel, J Tidy, W Van Panhuis, P Paul, C Emerson, J Grefenstette, R Wilder, A Herbst, D Heymann, D Burke, I Widjaya, D Winder, D Worrachaddejchai, Y Yee

Abstract:
Big data is already being used to measure, monitor, and manage tourism development, but its potential remains to be fully exploited. This report discusses the trends, opportunities, and challenges in using big data and digitalization in the tourism sector. It highlights how big data is being leveraged for COVID-19 recovery and examines its relationship with statistical frameworks to better measure the economic, social, and environmental impact of tourism. Case studies of partnerships in Asia and the Pacific between the public and private sector demonstrate ways to tap big data.
