Title: Experimental colitis promotes sustained, sex-dependent, T-cell-associated neuroinflammation and parkinsonian neuropathology
Authors: Madelyn C. Houser, W. Michael Caudle, Jianjun Chang, George T. Kannarkat, Yuan Yang, Sean D. Kelly, Danielle Oliver, Valerie Joers, Kathleen M. Shannon, Ali Keshavarzian, Malú G. Tansey
Year: 2021
Venue: Acta Neuropathologica Communications
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s40478-021-01240-4
Paper ID: https://openalex.org/W3196241139

Abstract:
BACKGROUND: The etiology of sporadic Parkinson's disease (PD) remains uncertain, but genetic, epidemiological, and physiological overlap between PD and inflammatory bowel disease suggests that gut inflammation could promote dysfunction of dopamine-producing neurons in the brain. Mechanisms behind this pathological gut-brain effect and their interactions with sex and with environmental factors are not well understood but may represent targets for therapeutic intervention. METHODS: T-cells were depleted. RESULTS: T-cell depletion prevented colitis-associated reductions in dopaminergic markers in males. In both sexes, colitis potentiated the effects of MPTP. RGS10 deficiency increased baseline intestinal inflammation, colitis severity, and neuropathology. CONCLUSIONS: T-cells in this process in male mice.
