Title: p21 induces a senescence program and skeletal muscle dysfunction
Authors: Davis A. Englund, Alyssa Jolliffe, Zaira Aversa, Xu Zhang, Ines Sturmlechner, Ayumi E. Sakamoto, Julianna D. Zeidler, Gina M. Warner, Colton McNinch, Thomas A. White, Eduardo N. Chini, Darren J. Baker, Jan M. van Deursen, Nathan K. LeBrasseur
Year: 2023
Venue: Molecular Metabolism
DOI: 10.1016/j.molmet.2022.101652
Paper ID: pmc_PMC9800630

Abstract:
Recent work has established associations between elevated p21, the accumulation of senescent cells, and skeletal muscle dysfunction in mice and humans. Using a mouse model of p21 overexpression (p21OE), we examined if p21 mechanistically contributes to cellular senescence and pathological features in skeletal muscle. We show that p21 induces several core properties of cellular senescence in skeletal muscle, including an altered transcriptome, DNA damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, and the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP). Furthermore, p21OE mice exhibit manifestations of skeletal muscle pathology, such as atrophy, fibrosis, and impaired physical function when compared to age-matched controls. These findings suggest p21 alone is sufficient to drive a cellular senescence program and reveal a novel source of skeletal muscle loss and dysfunction. Highlights • p21 induces a transcriptional program in skeletal muscle consistent with a senescence program. • p21 induces core properties of senescence in skeletal muscle, including DNA damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, and the SASP. • Mice that overexpress p21 exhibit signs of muscle pathology, such as atrophy, fibrosis, and impaired physical function.
