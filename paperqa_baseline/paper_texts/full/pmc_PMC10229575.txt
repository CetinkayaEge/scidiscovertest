Title: Fungal β-glucan-facilitated cross-feeding activities between Bacteroides and Bifidobacterium species
Authors: Pedro Fernandez-Julia, Gary W. Black, William Cheung, Douwe Van Sinderen, Jose Munoz-Munoz
Year: 2023
Venue: Communications Biology
DOI: 10.1038/s42003-023-04970-4
Paper ID: pmc_PMC10229575

Abstract:
The human gut microbiota (HGM) is comprised of a very complex network of microorganisms, which interact with the host thereby impacting on host health and well-being. β-glucan has been established as a dietary polysaccharide supporting growth of particular gut-associated bacteria, including members of the genera Bacteroides and Bifidobacterium , the latter considered to represent beneficial or probiotic bacteria. However, the exact mechanism underpinning β-glucan metabolism by gut commensals is not fully understood. We show that mycoprotein represents an excellent source for β-glucan, which is consumed by certain Bacteroides species as primary degraders, such as Bacteroides cellulosilyticus WH2. The latter bacterium employs two extracellular, endo-acting enzymes, belonging to glycoside hydrolase families 30 and 157, to degrade mycoprotein-derived β-glucan, thereby releasing oligosaccharides into the growth medium. These released oligosaccharides can in turn be utilized by other gut microbes, such as Bifidobacterium and Lactiplantibacillus , which thus act as secondary degraders. We used a cross-feeding approach to track how both species are able to grow in co-culture. ß-glucans, potential prebiotic fibers derived from mycoprotein, can be degraded by human gut Bacteroides via extracellular glycoside hydrolases; produced oligosaccharides can in turn feed Bifidobacterium species as shown by a cross-feeding approach.
