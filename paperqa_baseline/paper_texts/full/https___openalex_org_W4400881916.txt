Title: From CO2 to Sustainable Aviation Fuel: Navigating the Technology Landscape
Authors: Pussana Hirunsit, Alessandro Senocrate, Carlos E. Gómez-Camacho, Florian Kiefer
Year: 2024
Venue: ACS Sustainable Chemistry & Engineering
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.4c03939
Paper ID: https://openalex.org/W4400881916

Abstract:
Sustainable jet fuel plays a crucial role in reducing aviation’s carbon footprint, offering a promising approach toward net-zero emissions in the aviation sector. This work investigates pathways for producing jet fuels directly from CO&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt;. Given the early stage of many direct CO&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; utilization technologies, identifying promising pathways is essential. Our investigation focuses on the three most important routes for jet fuel production, each of which employs a distinct intermediate compound. These routes are the reverse water–gas shift and Fischer–Tropsch (RWGS-FT) route, the methanol route, and the CO&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; electrolysis route, which employ syngas, methanol, and ethylene as key intermediates, respectively. By performing comprehensive process simulations and analyzing the resulting energy intensity and thermal and CO&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; efficiency of each route, these findings provide quantitative early-stage evaluations and allow us to identify key technical development requirements. Our results indicate that the methanol route exhibits the lowest energy intensity, followed by the RWGS-FT and CO&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; electrolysis routes. H&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; production accounts for a significant share of the energy demand for the RWGS-FT and methanol routes. The RWGS-FT route shows the lowest CO&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; efficiency, while the methanol route achieves 92% CO&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; efficiency including recycle streams, highlighting its potential for jet fuel production. Furthermore, the CO&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; electrolysis route holds the potential to achieve close to 100% CO&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; efficiency and requires significantly less H&lt;sub&gt;2&lt;/sub&gt; feedstock. However, it faces challenges of a high energy demand. In addition, our study investigates key effects of potential technology optimization, providing a guideline for research and technology optimization.
