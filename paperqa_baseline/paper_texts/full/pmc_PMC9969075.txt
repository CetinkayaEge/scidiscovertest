Title: Human spinal GABA neurons survive and mature in the injured nonhuman primate spinal cord
Authors: Xiaolong Zheng, Bo Zhu, Jiang Xu, Dong Liu, Yan Huang, Daiqi Chen, Zhixian Liu, Fangliang Guo, Yuanji Dong, Wenzhen Zhu, Dengji Pan, Su-Chun Zhang, Hong Chen, Wei Wang
Year: 2023
Venue: Stem Cell Reports
DOI: 10.1016/j.stemcr.2022.12.016
Paper ID: pmc_PMC9969075

Abstract:
Summary Spinal cord injury (SCI) leads to permanent neural dysfunction without effective therapies. We previously showed that human pluripotent stem cell (hPSC)-derived spinal GABA neurons can alleviate spasticity and promote locomotion in rats after SCI, but whether this strategy can be translated into the clinic remains elusive. Here, a nonhuman primate (NHP) model of SCI was established in rhesus macaques ( Macaca mulatta ) in which the T10 spinal cord was hemisected, resulting in neural conduction failure and neural dysfunction, including locomotion deficits, pain, and spasms. Grafted human spinal GABA neurons survived for up to 7.5 months in the injured monkey spinal cord and retained their intrinsic properties, becoming mature and growing axons and forming synapses. Importantly, they are functionally alive, as evidenced by designer receptors exclusively activated by designer drug (DREADD) activation. These findings represent a significant step toward the clinical translation of human spinal neuron transplantation for treating SCI. Highlights • A nonhuman primate model of spinal cord injury is established in rhesus macaques • Human spinal GABA neurons survive and mature in the injured monkey spinal cord • Human spinal GABA neurons integrate into monkey neural circuits In this report, Wang and colleagues established a nonhuman primate model of spinal cord injury in rhesus macaques. Furthermore, transplanted human embryonic stem cell-derived spinal GABA neurons survived for a long time and became mature in the injured monkey spinal cord. Grafted human neurons were integrated into monkey neural circuits and can be activated by DREADD stimulation.
