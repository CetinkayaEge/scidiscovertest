Title: Mapping Education for Sustainability in the Nordic Countries
Authors: Ólafur Páll Jónsson, Bragi Guðmundsson, Anne Bergliot Øyehaug, Robert James Didham, Lili-Ann Wolff, Stefan Bengtsson, Jonas Andreasen Lysgaard, Bryndís Sóley Gunnarsdóttir, Sólveig María Árnadóttir, Jørgen Rømoen, Marianne Sund, Emelie Cockerell, Paul Plummer, Mathilda Brückner
Year: 2021
Venue: TemaNord
DOI: https://doi.org/10.6027/temanord2021-511
Paper ID: https://openalex.org/W3133964538

Abstract:
This report presents some of the main results of research conducted on Education&#13;\nfor Sustainable Development (ESD) in the Nordic countries – one of Iceland’s&#13;\npresidency projects for the Nordic Council of Ministers initiated in 2019 under the&#13;\nheading A Common Path (Nordic Council of Ministers, 2018). Iceland’s presidency&#13;\nfocused on the UN Sustainable Development Goals (UNSDGs) with special attention&#13;\nto young people. This was emphasised by the Prime Minister of Iceland and the&#13;\nMinister for Nordic Cooperation who introduced the projects.&#13;\nThe Icelandic Presidency will focus on issues concerning young people in the Nordic&#13;\nregion – the generation born around the turn of the century beginning to make its&#13;\nway in life. We want to listen to young people and support projects that promote&#13;\neducation, culture and health. (Nordic Council of Ministers, 2018, p. 5)&#13;\nThe project presented in this report concerns the implementation of UN Sustainable&#13;\nDevelopment Goal 4.7 in compulsory education in Denmark, Finland, Iceland,&#13;\nNorway, and Sweden. The aim was both to receive an overview of how well each of&#13;\nthe Nordic countries had integrated the UNSDGs into their educational policies and&#13;\npractices. There are seventeen UNSDGs; Goal 4 concerns education specifically. The&#13;\nsub-goal on which we focused our research was UNSDG 4.7 which states:&#13;\nBy 2030, ensure that all learners acquire the knowledge and skills needed to promote&#13;\nsustainable development, including, among others, through education for&#13;\nsustainable development and sustainable lifestyles, human rights, gender equality,&#13;\npromotion of a culture of peace and non-violence, global citizenship and&#13;\nappreciation of cultural diversity and of culture’s contribution to sustainable&#13;\ndevelopment.&#13;\nAs one can immediately see, the goal is broad and, therefore, a thorough mapping of&#13;\nits implementation was outside of the present project’s scope. However, we attempt&#13;\nto outline a picture of how things stand in the Nordic countries, looking at legislation,&#13;\ncurriculum, teacher education, and local implementation with the aim of identifying&#13;\na “Nordic Perspective”. We try to bring out not only how things stand in each&#13;\ncountry, but also to shed light on what is common and where things differ. The more&#13;\nwe have worked on this, the more we are painfully aware of how our work does little&#13;\nmore than scratch the surface. But in order to go deeper, one has to begin by&#13;\nscratching the surface. We hope that what we present here goes a little further than&#13;\nthe bare surface and, more importantly, will help others to continue to dig deeper.
